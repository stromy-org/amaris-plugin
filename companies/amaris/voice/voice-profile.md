# Amaris Consulting Voice Profile (L2)

Entity-specific voice for **Amaris Consulting**, layered on the L1 baseline
(`voice://baseline`). L2 adds to the floor; it never relaxes it.

Evidence labels: **[measured]** = seen directly in fetched page text;
**[judgement]** = inferred from a small sample. Sample: about a dozen amaris.com
and careers.amaris.com pages, English-heavy (see SOURCES.md).

## Language and register

- **English is the source register** on amaris.com. **[measured]** Insights,
  client stories, news and careers pages are English. The French site mirrors
  the nav and tagline.
- **French** uses collective **nous** and the formal register; the hero is
  "Réalisons cela ensemble." **[measured]** Depth of French body copy could not
  be read, so French long-form register is **[judgement]**: keep it plain,
  collective, vous-form, no anglicised hype.
- **Warmer to candidates.** The careers site addresses the reader as **you** and
  speaks as **we** ("your manager is your coach"). **[measured]**
- **Formal for compliance and regulatory news** (for example gender-equality
  index declarations), with a "proudly presents" register. **[measured]**

## Stance

A dependable craftsperson-partner: serious, passionate, never showy. The firm
presents itself as a guide who works alongside the client ("a client is just
another teammate"), not a vendor delivering a packaged solution. **[measured]**
Authority comes from named experts and concrete detail, not from slogans.
**[measured]** Editorial pieces often open with a human scene or a concrete
question, then move to substance. **[measured]**

## Do

- Write in plain **we** for firm voice and **you** for candidates and clients.
- Open expert pieces with a specific moment (a runner, an audit question, the gap
  between two assignments), then give the argument. **[measured]**
- Credit a named person (consultant, Center of Excellence head) for viewpoints.
  **[measured]**
- Structure client stories as: about the client, challenges faced, the path
  taken, benefits. **[measured]** Show collaboration (working sessions, feedback
  with the client's teams) before outcomes.
- Keep short declaratives for conviction: "That question is about to become
  standard." **[measured]**
- Use "Make it happen, together." as the tagline; the "together" idea recurs in
  the mission, culture and client language. **[measured]**
- Keep regulatory and technical precision where the audience is expert (GMP,
  MES, Annex terms), defining the term once. **[measured]**

## Do not

- Sound like a vendor: no supplier-distance language, no packaged-solution
  hype. **[judgement]**
- Use exclamation marks; none appeared in the pages read. **[measured]**
- Inflate with superlatives or self-awards in body copy. **[judgement]**
- Claim firm facts (founding year, headcount, country count, client count) from
  a voice anchor. Take them from `profile.json`.
- Use unqualified AI enthusiasm in regulated contexts; viewpoint pieces keep the
  human accountable ("the Quality Unit keeps its responsibility"). **[measured]**

## Vocabulary the brand owns

`Make it happen, together`; `positive agents of change`; `craftspeople`;
`dependable and trusted partners`; `go the extra mile`; `teammate`;
`intrapreneur`; `Centers of Excellence`; `Global Change Agent`. **[measured]**
Sector names used as given: Life Sciences, Information Systems & Digital,
Telecom, Engineering.

## Do the stated values appear in real copy?

Values on file: Pioneer Spirit, Trust, Care, Performance, Independence.

- **Named verbatim** on amaris.com/about-us as the five core values, and a
  French rendering (Esprit pionnier, Confiance, Bienveillance or "se soucier",
  Performance, Indépendance) appeared in search snippets. **[measured, with the
  French form secondhand]**
- **Lived in the prose?** Unevenly. **Care** and **Trust** show up in practice
  ("We care about our clients' projects like our own"; "trusted with
  transformative projects from day one"). **Independence** appears as the
  self-description "independent". **Performance** appears as "excellence" more
  than the word itself. **Pioneer Spirit** is the thinnest: the pages read lean
  on "intrapreneur" and "change agent" instead, and none of the values are
  chanted as slogans in articles. **[judgement]** Use the five as an internal
  checklist, not as headline copy.
- Note: groupamaris.com (values Confiance, Proximité, Agilité) is a different
  French engineering company and was not used.

## How it varies by surface

| Surface | Register |
|---|---|
| Home / about | Short, warm, collective we; mission in why/who/how |
| Insights, viewpoint | Expert, scene-first, third person with named author |
| Insights, people stories | Narrative profile, direct invitation to the reader |
| Client stories | Problem, approach, benefit; professional, collaborative |
| News / press | Corporate and factual; executive quote; formal |
| Careers | You/we, empowering, coaching metaphors |
| Compliance notices | Formal declaration language |

## Relationship to L1

L1 already bans em-dashes, antithesis frames, filler openers and overused
vocabulary. This profile adds the partner-not-vendor stance, scene-first
editorial openings, the you/we split for careers, and hype bans.

## Social register (measured from 40 X posts and 27 LinkedIn posts, captured 2026-10-06)

Counts below were hand-tallied from the fetched items (not scripted); treat as +/-1. Only company-account posts counted. X: 40 of 40 (no retweets/replies appeared). LinkedIn: 30 fetched, 27 used (1 employee post and 2 company reshares with a repostId excluded).

**Staleness caveat (MEASURED).** The X account @Amaris is dormant: all 40 posts fall between 2024-05-07 and 2024-08-08, nothing newer. The LinkedIn sample runs 2026-06-10 to 2026-10-05. Treat X as a historical, thin-register source and LinkedIn as the live voice.

### How social differs from the corporate site (JUDGEMENT, grounded in the samples)
- LinkedIn is the human register: a short declarative hook, then people, events and client contexts, with named individuals credited (about 16 of 27 posts name a colleague or speaker). Corporate copy is firm-centred; social is people-centred.
- X is a thin link-teaser register: one or two sentences, "At Amaris Consulting, we ...", then "read our article" plus a link. It is the most corporate of the two channels.
- Neither channel is chatty. No jokes, no slang, no questions to the audience on X.

### Post shapes and length
- X (MEASURED): 40 posts, typically 1 to 3 short sentences plus a link; 7 open with the literal "At Amaris Consulting," and most others start with a topic noun or an announcement marker (a megaphone emoji appears in 5).
- LinkedIn (MEASURED): 27 posts, mostly 100 to 250 words, built as a one-line hook, short paragraphs, then a call to action. 6 use Unicode bold headlines; 15 or more open with a one-line hook that reframes (for example "X is more than Y", "More tests don't always mean better testing"). Recurrent shapes: employee story (6), event recap or invitation (7), recognition or award (3), leadership appointment or joiners (3), thought-leadership or product (6).

### Opening moves (MEASURED / JUDGEMENT)
- LinkedIn hooks are claim-then-turn: a short principle followed by "Because ..." reasoning (JUDGEMENT; seen in 8+ posts).
- Numbers-led openings appear 3 times (a short count, then a plain claim, e.g. "500 test cases. Under 15 minutes.").
- X: self-naming opener "At Amaris Consulting, we ..." in 7 of 40.

### Hashtags, emoji, mentions
- Hashtags: X 14 of 40 posts carry real hashtags (usually #AmarisConsulting plus a topic tag); LinkedIn 24 of 27, typically 3 to 6 at the foot, #AmarisConsulting in most.
- Emoji: X about 24 of 40 posts carry at least one (rocket, globe, megaphone, pointing hand); LinkedIn 14 of 27, used as sparse punctuation (pointing-down for "link in the first comment", clap, rocket).
- Mentions: X has 1 @mention in 40; LinkedIn credits named colleagues in prose rather than with tags.
- The firm signs itself "Amaris Consulting | Part of Mantu" in 8 of 27 LinkedIn posts, none on X (2024).

### Language split (MEASURED)
- X: English 35, French 3, Spanish 1, Portuguese 1. French is used for Switzerland and Canada event and trade-show posts (register: formal "nous", institutional, no emoji except the megaphone-led event call).
- LinkedIn: English 25, Spanish 1 (a Colombia data-engineer story, "tu" form, warm and direct), English plus Japanese 1. No French post in the 27.
- The company page is a single global account; the language follows the local market of the story, not a translation of one master post.

### Cadence and topics (MEASURED)
- X: 40 posts in about 13 weeks (May to Aug 2024), roughly 3 per week, then silence. Topics: client stories and articles, Centers of Excellence, internships and careers, awards, events.
- LinkedIn: 27 posts in about 17 weeks, roughly 1.5 per week. Topics: employee and career stories, events and webinars, AI and software-quality product (AMAIA-QA), Workday delivery, recognitions, leadership appointments.

### Calls to action (MEASURED)
- X: nearly every post ends in a link; "read our article / learn more / find out more" in about 17 of 40.
- LinkedIn: "link in the first comment" or "in the comments" in 8 of 27; a few invite demo contact or an open-roles click; 1 ends with an audience question.

### Values on social (MEASURED words, JUDGEMENT on fit)
- Trust: lived 4 of 27 on LinkedIn, almost always as thanks to clients after a recognition ("trust" used 4 times) and 1 time on X.
- Performance: lived through concrete results (an ETL run cut from nearly 3 hours to 30-40 minutes, 500 test cases in under 15 minutes, a 10-month Workday delivery). Numbers appear in about 8 of 27.
- Care: shows in people stories, volunteering, wellbeing recognition and "welcome" posts (about 8 of 27); never as a word like "care".
- Pioneer Spirit: shows as "innovation" (about 5 of 40 X posts) and as internally built AI products on LinkedIn; the word "pioneer" appears 0 times.
- Independence: effectively absent. 0 posts use "independence" or "independent"; one profile story mentions "real autonomy". Do not expect social copy to carry this value.

### What the brand never does on social (MEASURED absence)
- No humour or memes, no slang, no profanity.
- No pricing, no competitor naming, no rhetorical "Imagine..." openers in the sample.
- No first-person-singular company voice ("I"); the voice is "we" or the firm named in third person.
- No hard-sell language; sales intent is soft ("get in touch to schedule a demo").

### Caveats
- X sample comes from a single 13-week window in 2024; do not treat its tone as current.
- Small sample, one page per platform, LinkedIn likes/comments not analysed. Counts hand-tallied.
- Some LinkedIn posts use Unicode bold, which is a rendering trick; reproduce with plain text or markdown bold, not Unicode letters.
